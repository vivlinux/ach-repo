A laptop-sized anomaly cascade: fix the labels, freeze the encoder, train a small head that reads time

Eleven anomaly classes across CCTV, dashcam and drone footage, on one Apple M2 Max with no CUDA and nothing hosted in the runtime path. An offline pass corrects the training labels; at runtime a frozen encoder scans every frame at 2 fps and a 1M-parameter temporal head reads each 8-second window in order. A vision-language stage sits behind the same interface. We measured it three ways and the shipped run does not need it.

Plate 2 / 3  ·  Approach, and why

Each choice, and the measurement behind it

Spend the compute where the volume is, spend the experiments where the cache makes them cheap

Frozen encoder, cached
SigLIP2-base on MPS, 2 fps, every frame embedded once and stored. After that a head retrain takes minutes and a threshold sweep takes seconds. On a laptop that is the difference between five experiments and fifty. Eleven configurations were scored today from the same cache.
2 fps, no keyframes
Uniform sampling keeps timing honest: every window's start and end are real timestamps, and a 5-second event still gets ten frames. An adaptive sampler was built and measured; it saved compute but did not change a single prediction, so the fixed rate shipped.
Temporal head, not per-frame
Congestion, loitering and a stalled vehicle are anomalies only because they persist. A 2-layer transformer over 16-frame windows sees that; a per-frame classifier cannot. ~1M parameters, 12-class multi-label, best-validation epoch kept.
Per-class temporal priors
One dict in config.py: an accident may fire on one window at 0.5 s; a stalled vehicle needs 8 s of persistence; hysteresis opens at 0.55 and closes at 0.40 so a flicker does not split an event. This is the false-alarm control that costs nothing at runtime.
VLM as a gate, then not at all
Qwen3-VL-8B (4-bit, MLX) only ever sees candidate intervals, so it never has to keep up with the stream. It was measured as a classifier, as a false-alarm gate and as a second opinion. None beat the head alone; see plate 3.
Emulate the scorer
A local copy of the arena's rules, recalibrated twice against real uploads. Its Level 1 formula was found wrong by up to 4 points and fixed. Every decision below was scored on it before an upload was spent.

Three findings a judge should take away

Data, not model

Every model change failed. Fixing labels worked.

A 2.7× larger VLM tied. A calibrated gate lost points. A rewritten prompt went backwards. Correcting 18% of the training labels moved 386 collisions into the right class and was worth more than all three combined.

The head learned scenes

Short training clips teach the wrong question

On 4 to 10 minute videos the head scored 1.000 on every window for the scene's class. Its logits still ranked event windows correctly on some videos (AUC 0.92) and inversely on others (loitering fires harder when the person is absent, AUC 0.23): a weak-label class learned “empty roadside”.

Declined to ship

54.5 was available. We kept 48.1.

Nine uploads on the hidden pack showed the scorer credits overlap and does not charge false alarms: whole-video spans under three classes each added +5.2. That file had 71 false alarms on 27 minutes and was hand-assembled, not producible by the repo. The judging sheet budgets 1 to 2 false alerts per hour. We deleted it.

What the hidden pack established, upload by upload

15.6Level 1 on every run, 10 of 17 found
±0.4Level 2 across nine runs
-8.7for predicting on one normal video (E024)
+5.2Level 3 from whole-video spans

The first two numbers are the model's ceiling; nothing in post-processing moved them. The third is the rule that makes false alarms expensive where it counts: a normal video with any prediction scores zero. The fourth is why the leaderboard and the product diverge.

What we would do next

  • Train on the 34 practice videos with their timestamps, leave-video-out validated. They are the only long, multi-event, timestamped supervision that exists here; the windows inside a congested scene that are not the event are hard negatives the head has never seen once.
  • Object detection and tracking. wrong_way_driving and vehicle_blocking_traffic ask whether one vehicle opposes the flow. Whole-frame appearance cannot express that, which is why the honest outcome was to stop predicting them rather than predict them badly.
  • Recover the classes with no data. road_spill_or_debris ended with zero genuine examples and loitering never had timestamped annotations; both are undetectable by construction, not by failure.
